# Prime survival of holes to N = 10^8 (2026-11-25)

Command:

    python experiments/src/k_survival_1e8.py --terms 100000000 --workdir <scratch> --top 30

- **Generator.** The sequence is generated by a C helper, `experiments/src/k_survival_1e8_gen.c`, compiled automatically with gcc. It sieves to 2.0e8 and stores int32 arrays. 10^8 terms take 9 s, and the whole run takes about 28 s.
- **Outputs.** Raw output is in `stdout.txt`. The binary outputs (about 1.4 GB) are not kept; rerun the command to regenerate them.
- **Code checks.**
  - The first 20 terms match A055265.
  - Agreement with greedy.py up to 10^6.
  - H_p matches a brute-force count below 2·10^7.
  - Below 10^7, the earlier K and C009' counts are reproduced exactly.
  - All eight OEIS |a(n) - n| records up to 10^8 (A282696/A282697) match, and there are no extra records between them.

## Definition

K(v) = π(fi(v) - 1 + v) - π(2v) is the number of primes hole v survives (fi(v) > v).

## Conclusions

1. **Measured.** The "K0 = 8" suggested by the data up to 10^7 fails: 9 first appears at v = 15.4M, 10 at 43.9M and 11 at 76.1M. At fixed sample size the blocks above 10^7 reach 9-11, so the tail is getting heavier, not just the sample growing.
2. **Measured.** C009' holds without exception. Once its right side is >= 20, K is at most half of it.
3. **Interpretation.** A geometric tail with ratio about 0.16, drifting slowly upward, predicts that max K grows by about 1.2 per decade, which is what we see. There is no sign of a cutoff. A uniform bound **K = O(log v)** is better supported than an absolute K0, so it is the realistic proof target. It suffices for the permutation property.
4. **Interpretation.** Large K comes from prime-dense windows combined with large H, not mainly from prime gaps. A proof must control how many of the payers are prime partners of v, not just how many payers there are.
