# OEIS A253074 (and A253073) - the "non-prime sum" analogue

## Citation/link

https://oeis.org/A253074 ; companion https://oeis.org/A253073.

## What it says (from search snippets only)

- A253074: lexicographically earliest sequence of distinct nonnegative integers with a(n-1) + a(n) **not** prime. Conjectured (or proved?) to be a permutation of the nonnegative integers.
- The entry contains a **proof outline credited to Patrick Devlin and Semeon Artamonov**: if x never appears, then (since x would otherwise be chosen) every sufficiently late term t satisfies t + x prime, i.e. every late term is of the form (prime - x); then fix a prime p not dividing x and look at residues mod p of consecutive shifted terms to force one of them to be divisible by p, a contradiction.
- A253073 is a companion sequence (conjectured permutation of the nonprimes) with a similar argument.

## Relevance

**Closest prior argument found for C003/C004.** The first step is the same "smallest missing value constrains all late terms" trick as C003 (here: every late a(n) of the right parity has a(n) + m composite) and the "values p - m are then excluded" observation behind C004. In A253074 the constraint is strong enough to finish (late terms all lie in {prime - x}, a thin set); in our sequence the constraint is the opposite (late terms avoid {prime - m}), which only yields C004's density statement, not a contradiction. So C003/C004 should be presented as an instance of a known, folklore-style technique (cite this entry and Lagarias-Rains-Sloane), not as new. C005 (the counting reduction) was not seen anywhere, but it is a routine consequence.
