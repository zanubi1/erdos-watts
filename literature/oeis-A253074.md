# OEIS A253074 - the "non-prime sum" analogue (Devlin-Artamonov argument)

## Citation/link

https://oeis.org/A253074 (read via https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/main/seq/A253/A253074.seq). N. J. A. Sloane, Feb 01 2015, based on a suggestion of Patrick Devlin. Related: A055266 ("essentially the same", Mathar), A253073.

## What it says

- %N: "Lexicographically earliest sequence of distinct numbers such that a(n-1)+a(n) is not prime."
- "Conjecture: this is a permutation of the nonnegative numbers." This is followed by a "[Proof outline below due to Patrick Devlin and Semeon Artamonov]":
  - If x is missing, then "eventually every term must be of the form PRIME - x. (Otherwise, x would appear as that next term.)"
  - So only finitely many multiples of x appear. Take Y beyond them and a prime q not dividing Y. Then Y, 2Y, ..., 2qY are all missing, so late terms t have t + kY prime for all k <= 2q. Reducing mod q gives a contradiction.
- Hasler (Nov 25 2019) adds a "Simplified version of the proof" along the same lines, using m·x for k+1 <= m <= k+p.
- The entry still labels the statement "Conjecture", although the outline appears to settle it.

## Relevance

**Closest prior argument found for C003/C004.** Its first step, "the smallest missing value x is an admissible candidate at every late step, so every late term avoids/satisfies the relation with x", is exactly the mechanism of C003. Its second step, "therefore other specific values (multiples of x) are also missing", is the same move as C004 (there: values p - m are missing).

The difference is that in A253074 the forced relation ("late term + x is prime") puts all late terms in a thin set, which gives a contradiction. In A055265 the forced relation is the complement ("late term + m is composite"), which only yields C004's density bound. So C003/C004 should be cited as instances of this known technique (also EKG, see lagarias-rains-sloane-2002.md), not presented as new. I saw no counterpart of C005 here.
