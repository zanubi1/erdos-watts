# OEIS A055265 (and its cross-references)

**Link:** https://oeis.org/A055265 . Read via the OEIS data repository mirror on GitHub: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oeis/oeisdata/main/seq/A055/A055265.seq (same text as the OEIS entry; oeis.org itself was unreachable).

## What it says (A055265, read 2026-11-24)

- %N: "a(n) is the smallest positive integer not already in the sequence such that a(n)+a(n-1) is prime, starting with a(1)=1."
- Well defined: terms alternate in parity, and Dirichlet gives existence (Sloane, Mar 07 2017).
- "Does every positive integer eventually occur?" (Kamenetsky, May 27 2009). Wilson v: "almost certainly yes, on probabilistic grounds." **No proof in the entry.**
- Appears to be the limit of the rows of A051237; "no proof is known for either conjecture" (Wilson v, 2011).
- Hasler (Feb 11 2020): case M = 2, N = 1 of "among the pairwise sums of any M consecutive terms, N are prime"; for (2, 0) see A055266 and A253074. Formula: a(2n-1) = A128280(2n-1) - 1, a(2n) = A128280(2n) + 1.
- %Y: inverse A117922; fixed points A117925; A117923 = a(a(n)); A086527 the primes a(n)+a(n-1); A070942; "See also A076990, A243625."; "See A282695 for deviation from identity sequence."; A073659 partial-sum variant.
- The entry is listed in the index of permutations of the natural numbers, although that property is unproven. Keywords: easy, nice, nonn. Author Henry Bottomley, May 09 2000.

## Cross-references (all read via the oeisdata GitHub mirror, 2026-11-24)

- **A282695** = A055265(n) - n (Sloane, Feb 2017). Comment: "There is no proof at present for the conjecture that A055265 is a permutation of the positive integers." Also: "remarkably small deviations from the identity sequence." b-file has 10^4 terms.

  **This matches our own runs exactly** (156 at 10^6, 200 at n ≈ 8,526,046 for 10^7), which independently checks our code. The largest recorded deviation is 2·197 = 394, at a position beyond 1.08·10^8 that I did not see. (The fetch tool may have cut off the last 3 positions of A282696.) These are numerical records only; no bound is proved.
- **A070942**: "Values of n such that the first n terms of A055265 constitute a permutation of {1,2,3,...,n}." (Layman, 2003). Data: 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 17, 18, ..., 653, 654, 665. No comments, and no claim that it is infinite.
- **A076990**: *not* a displacement sequence. It is a different greedy sequence: a(1)=1, a(2)=2, a(n) = least unused number such that the sum of **three** successive terms is prime (Murthy 2002). Comments give empirical growth only (Wilson v, Israel, Hasler). Our earlier index wrongly listed it as "deviation from identity".
- **A243625**: *not* a displacement sequence either. It is the greedy sequence with a(n)+a(n-1) **semiprime**, a(1)=1 (Lagneau 2014). The comment says "It is probable that every positive integer occurs".
- **A128280**: the a(1)=2 variant (not read beyond the formula above).

## Relevance

- Confirms that the permutation property is **open** in OEIS (A282695's comment says so explicitly).
- No proved displacement bound anywhere. The empirical records reach 394 near n ~ 10^8, consistent with (log n)^2 growth: (ln 1.08e8)^2 ≈ 342 versus a record of 316 there. L005 needs only o(n/log n).
