# OEIS A055265

**Link:** https://oeis.org/A055265 - "a(n) is the smallest positive integer not already in the sequence such that a(n)+a(n-1) is prime, starting with a(1)=1."

## What it says (paraphrase)

- Well defined; terms alternate in parity (Sloane, 2017).
- Kamenetsky (2009) asked whether every positive integer occurs; R. G. Wilson v: almost certainly yes on probabilistic grounds. **No proof given.**
- Appears to be the limit of the rows of A051237 (unproven).
- Hasler (2020): the case M = 2, N = 1 of "among the pairwise sums of any M consecutive terms, N are prime"; OEIS wiki page "Prime sums from neighboring terms".
- Cross-references: inverse permutation A117922, fixed points A117925, a(a(n)) A117923, the prime sums A086527, n such that a(1..n) is a permutation of 1..n: A070942, deviation from identity: A076990, A243625, A282695.
- b-files: 10^4 terms (Seidov), 10^5 terms (Sloane).
